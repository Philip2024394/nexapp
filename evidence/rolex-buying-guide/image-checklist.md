# Rolex Buying Guide — Image Checklist

All images are owner-supplied photographs. No AI-generated imagery. No stock photography misrepresented as the owner's own. No implication that any workshop or factory photo was taken inside a Rolex facility unless the owner actually took it there and has the rights to publish it. All images are square 1:1 unless noted; square makes the gallery render predictably in both the phone-frame NEX surface and any wider future layout.

Each image corresponds to a specific paragraph in `content-notes.md`. If a photo cannot be sourced, the implementation agent should hide the image slot rather than substitute a different image — the guide should not fabricate illustration.

---

### Image 1 — Hero / Section header
- **Aspect:** Square 1:1
- **Subject:** A single Rolex sitting flat against a plain, non-reflective surface (matte grey, oak, or dark linen). Owner's own watch. The crown should be visible at 3 o'clock, bracelet laid flat, no visible dust or lint. Natural daylight preferred.
- **Why this slot:** Anchors the top of the page and signals "this is a product-focused guide, not a lifestyle shoot." The flat-on-surface framing avoids any suggestion of a Rolex advertisement.

### Image 2 — History section opener
- **Aspect:** Square 1:1
- **Subject:** A clean photograph of a vintage Rolex (any era pre-1990), ideally showing an Oyster-style case profile and an older-style dial. Owner's own piece or a borrowed piece with written permission to publish. If no vintage piece is available, this slot should be omitted rather than substituted.
- **Why this slot:** Supports the History paragraph covering 1905-1953 (H1-H9). Gives the reader a visual bridge from "brand founded in London in 1905" to "modern references" without needing archival imagery the publisher does not own.

### Image 3 — Oyster case close-up
- **Aspect:** Square 1:1
- **Subject:** Close-up of a screw-down crown (preferably Twinlock or Triplock, with the Rolex crown logo visible) and the edge of the case back. Lighting should be raking / side-angled to show the threading.
- **Why this slot:** Supports the 1926 Oyster-case paragraph (H5) and the water-resistance paragraph in Section 2 (B4). Lets the reader see the engineering claim with their own eyes.

### Image 4 — Movement visible under caseback (optional)
- **Aspect:** Square 1:1
- **Subject:** A Rolex automatic movement visible through an off-case photograph — watchmaker-supplied or owner-supplied. The rotor should be clearly in frame. Rolex production watches do not use display backs, so this image must be a legitimate service photograph, not a mock-up. If no legitimate movement photo is available, omit.
- **Why this slot:** Supports the Perpetual rotor paragraph (H6) and the Parachrom / Chronergy section (M5, M7). Also helps the authentication paragraph (B7) which hinges on the idea that only movement inspection is reliable.

### Image 5 — Manufacturing / material detail
- **Aspect:** Square 1:1
- **Subject:** Close-up of an Oystersteel bracelet link, showing the brushed-and-polished finishing and the lug end-link transition. Owner's own watch. Macro lens if available.
- **Why this slot:** Supports the 904L / Oystersteel paragraph (M1, M2, M3) without needing any factory-floor photograph the publisher does not own.

### Image 6 — Sizing comparison on wrist
- **Aspect:** Square 1:1
- **Subject:** Wrist shot showing a 40-41 mm Rolex worn, with the lug-to-lug clearly landing within the wrist width. The wrist should be shown from above, bracelet taper visible. Include a note of wrist circumference in the caption (e.g. "17.5 cm wrist, Submariner Date 41 mm").
- **Why this slot:** Directly supports the sizing paragraph (B6) and the "try before you buy" guidance.

### Image 7 — Second sizing reference (smaller case)
- **Aspect:** Square 1:1
- **Subject:** Wrist shot of a 36 mm Rolex (Datejust 36, Explorer 36, or Oyster Perpetual 36). Same framing conventions as Image 6. Caption notes wrist circumference and model.
- **Why this slot:** Allows the reader to compare 36 mm vs 41 mm proportions directly. Supports B6.

### Image 8 — Water-resistance context
- **Aspect:** Square 1:1
- **Subject:** A modern Submariner or Sea-Dweller photographed near (not submerged in) water — on a boat deck, a pier plank, or a pool edge. No underwater photography. Keeps the imagery honest to the lab-rated nature of the claim.
- **Why this slot:** Supports the water-resistance paragraph (B4, B5) and the Submariner / Sea-Dweller history paragraph (H8, H12). The "near water, not under water" framing matches the paragraph's point that the rating is a laboratory specification, not a lifetime guarantee.

### Image 9 — Full set documentation
- **Aspect:** Square 1:1
- **Subject:** A flat-lay of a Rolex with its "full set" — green outer card box, inner presentation box, warranty card, hang tags, instruction booklet, and service pouch if available. Owner's own set.
- **Why this slot:** Supports the Section 4 paragraph on resale factors (MV6, MV7). Lets the reader see what "box and papers" actually means.

### Image 10 — Service documentation
- **Aspect:** Square 1:1
- **Subject:** A Rolex Service Centre service receipt or service booklet, redacted for personal information (owner name, serial number). The Rolex crown and the service date should be visible.
- **Why this slot:** Supports the service-interval and service-impact-on-resale paragraphs (B1, MV10). Makes the "documented service history" claim concrete.

### Image 11 — CPO seal / packaging (if available)
- **Aspect:** Square 1:1
- **Subject:** A Rolex Certified Pre-Owned seal or the CPO-specific packaging / pouch from a CPO purchase. Owner-sourced. If the publisher has not purchased a CPO watch, omit rather than substitute.
- **Why this slot:** Supports the CPO paragraph (MV8). Makes the "what you actually get with CPO" claim visually concrete.

### Image 12 — Authentication / caseback (closing image)
- **Aspect:** Square 1:1
- **Subject:** A watchmaker's hands removing a Rolex caseback on a bench — the loupe, case-holder and movement tray should be visible. Taken in an independent watchmaker's workshop with permission to publish, or in the owner's own workspace if they do this themselves. Should not be staged to look like a Rolex Service Centre.
- **Why this slot:** Closes the Buying & Ownership section on the authentication paragraph (B7). Makes the "no online check beats physical movement inspection" point visually, and prevents the guide from ending on market-value / money framing — the final visual is about craft and verification, not about resale price.
