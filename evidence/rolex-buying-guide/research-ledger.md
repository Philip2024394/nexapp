**Access window:** 2026-11-17 (single research session).
**Tool availability note:** WebFetch was denied by the harness during this session (permission denied on first call). All evidence therefore comes from WebSearch result snippets and the search tool's summarisation layer. Primary Rolex.com pages could not be opened directly; where only Rolex.com was cited by the search tool, that claim is flagged in the "Manufacturer-only claims" sub-section. Google was accessible through the WebSearch tool, but SERP features (People-Also-Ask boxes, related-queries, autocomplete) were not programmatically returned — only ranked result titles/URLs and a synthesised answer. Where I describe a question as "google-observed" in `questions-30.md`, I mean I confirmed the question phrasing appears as (a) an indexed article title or (b) an evident SERP cluster around that wording from the WebSearch response, not that I harvested it from a live PAA widget.

**Currency / period note:** every market-value row below states currency, region, and the period the figure was cited for. None of these figures should be forwarded into the reader-facing guide without the surrounding caveat that completed sales data is dealer-reported, not independently audited, and that a future reader will be looking at older data.

## Manufacturer-only claims (flagged, not independently verified at primary source)

These claims trace back only to Rolex-published material (rolex.com, newsroom.rolex.com, Rolex PDFs) and were seen only via WebSearch summarisation during this session (WebFetch was denied). They should be either (a) written as "Rolex states that…" / "according to Rolex…" in reader-facing copy, or (b) independently re-verified before any strong factual claim is made.

- **Oyster case patent narrative** — the "world's first waterproof wristwatch" claim is Rolex's own framing; perezcope.com argues the claim is inaccurate as framed. Guide should say "the first commercially successful waterproof wristwatch case."
- **Perpetual rotor as the "world's first" self-winding wristwatch** — Rolex framing. Automatic wristwatch work by John Harwood predates Rolex's Perpetual patent (Harwood patented a bumper automatic in 1923; it was not a free-rotating 360° rotor). The Rolex claim "world's first self-winding **with a Perpetual rotor**" is accurate as narrowly worded; the broader "first self-winding wristwatch" claim is not.
- **Superlative Chronometer -2/+2 sec/day in-house testing** — Rolex publishes the standard in its 2024 brochure (M4). Third-party testing of fleet accuracy exists but was not pulled in this session.
- **Oystersteel composition and in-house foundry workflow** — the specific percentages and the Plan-les-Ouates foundry claim come from editorial sources summarising Rolex press material. No third-party metallurgical audit was located during this session.
- **Submariner 300 m / +10% pressure test** — the Rolex Submariner user-guide PDF states the 300 m rating. The "+10% over rated depth" test figure is editorial and ultimately traces to Rolex communications.
- **Everest 1953 "Rolex on the summit"** — contested. Rolex equipped the expedition; Norgay wore a Rolex; Hillary's own summit watch is historically uncertain. Guide should reflect this nuance (H9).
