## The four owner-named questions

### 1. Do Rolex watches lose value?
- **Origin:** `sourced-elsewhere` — question is used verbatim as the article title on https://brite.co/blog/do-rolex-watches-hold-their-value/, https://majestixcollection.com/blog/do-rolex-watches-hold-their-value/, and https://www.watch-affinity.com/blog/do-rolex-watches-hold-value (observed 2026-11-17 via WebSearch result titles).
- **Short answer:** Yes — most Rolex watches lose some value the moment they leave an authorised dealer, and the drop in year one is typically in the low double digits of percent on a broad basket basis. But "lose value" is not the whole story: aggregated secondary-market data over multi-year windows shows popular steel sports references (Submariner, GMT-Master II, Daytona) trading at or above retail, while entry-level dress models and precious-metal pieces are the most likely to sit below retail on the pre-owned market. The direction of travel depends on model, market period, and condition, not on the badge alone.
- **Evidence keys:** MV1, MV2, MV4, MV5, MV6.

### 2. Which factors affect resale value?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion` (standard reader-facing framing; corroborated by https://www.diamondbanc.com/sell-jewelry/watches/rolex/what-makes-a-rolex-expensive/).
- **Short answer:** The dominant drivers reported across dealer and auction data are, in roughly descending order of impact: model and reference (sports steel outperforms dress and precious metal on secondary demand), condition (unpolished case, sharp lugs, clean dial), completeness ("full set" — box, warranty card, booklets, tags, service records), documented provenance (purchase receipt and prior service history), age/rarity and market period, and finally dealer vs private-sale channel (dealers typically take a spread for warranty and authentication).
- **Evidence keys:** MV6, MV7, MV10, B7.

### 3. Does buying at retail versus resale change the outcome?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion` (owner-named phrasing).
- **Short answer:** Yes, and the change is model-specific. If you can get an authorised-dealer allocation for a popular steel sports reference, you pay retail and avoid the secondary-market spread — on in-demand references this has historically meant immediate paper value above what you paid. For entry-level or precious-metal references, the pre-owned market typically sits below retail, so buying new means absorbing depreciation that a careful pre-owned buyer avoids. The hidden variables are (a) dealer waitlist time (which has a real opportunity cost), (b) warranty — new gets five years, Rolex Certified Pre-Owned gets two — and (c) grey-market premium, which has compressed in 2024-2026 for most models but remains very large on Daytona.
- **Evidence keys:** MV1, MV2, MV3, MV6, MV8.

### 4. What does servicing cost mean for returns?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion` (owner-named phrasing).
- **Short answer:** Servicing is a holding cost that reduces any gross price appreciation into a net return. At Rolex Service Centre pricing a modern three-hand service is in the ~$660-$940 USD or ~£550-£780 GBP band in 2026, and the service is recommended roughly every ten years on modern references. Documented service history can support a modestly higher asking price when selling — commonly cited as a 10-20% uplift over a comparable undocumented watch — but that uplift rarely exceeds the service's own cost, so the honest framing is that servicing protects value rather than adding it. On vintage and collectible pieces, standard refinishing during service can actually reduce value; owners of such pieces typically request "service without polishing."
- **Evidence keys:** B1, B2, B3, MV10.

## History & brand questions

### 5. When was Rolex founded, and by whom?
- **Origin:** `google-observed` — query "when was Rolex founded" surfaced consistent article titles including "History of Rolex: Founded 1905" (https://corpdigest.com/company/rolex/company-history), observed 2026-11-17.
- **Short answer:** The firm was founded as Wilsdorf and Davis in London in 1905 by Hans Wilsdorf (a German national) and Alfred Davis (a British watch dealer, and later Wilsdorf's brother-in-law). The "Rolex" trademark was registered in 1908, and the company relocated to Geneva in 1919 largely in response to post-war British taxation on luxury imports.
- **Evidence keys:** H1, H2, H3.

### 6. Why is Rolex based in Geneva?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion` (context for H3).
- **Short answer:** Rolex moved from London to Geneva in 1919 because the post-war British tax regime made importing Swiss movements and exporting cased watches (with silver and gold) prohibitively expensive. Montres Rolex SA was registered in Geneva in 1920, and the Acacias-district headquarters has been Rolex's home since 1965.
- **Evidence keys:** H3, H15.

### 7. What was the Kew Observatory certificate of 1914?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion` (pivotal milestone).
- **Short answer:** On 15 July 1914, a Rolex wristwatch was awarded a Class "A" precision certificate by Kew Observatory in Great Britain — a rating that had previously been reserved for marine chronometers. The watch passed 45 days of testing in five positions and three temperature conditions. This was a watershed moment because it demonstrated that a wristwatch could match or exceed the precision of pocket watches and marine chronometers of the era.
- **Evidence keys:** H4.

### 8. When did Rolex introduce the Oyster (waterproof) case?
- **Origin:** `google-observed` — query "rolex oyster case 1926 history" clustered around article titles with that date (observed 2026-11-17).
- **Short answer:** The Oyster case was patented and launched in 1926-1927. The core innovation was sealing the movement by screwing the bezel, case back and winding crown against the middle case. It was the first *commercially successful* waterproof wristwatch case — there were earlier experimental waterproof cases, but the Oyster was the one that moved production at scale.
- **Evidence keys:** H5.

### 9. When did Rolex introduce the Perpetual self-winding rotor?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion`.
- **Short answer:** The free-rotating Perpetual rotor was patented by Rolex in 1931. The innovation was the use of a semicircular weight that rotated a full 360° in either direction and wound the mainspring continuously as the wrist moved, replacing earlier "bumper"-style automatic mechanisms that only wound in limited arcs.
- **Evidence keys:** H6.

### 10. When was the Datejust introduced?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion`.
- **Short answer:** The Datejust was introduced in 1945, to mark Rolex's 40th anniversary, and is widely credited as the first wristwatch with an automatically changing date in a dial window.
- **Evidence keys:** H7.

### 11. When was the Submariner introduced?
- **Origin:** `google-observed` — query "rolex submariner history 1953" clustered around multiple article titles with that date (observed 2026-11-17).
- **Short answer:** The Submariner debuted in 1953, with the first production reference (6204) shown at the Basel fair in 1954. It was conceived as a professional dive watch and quickly became the reference point for the modern dive-watch genre.
- **Evidence keys:** H8.

### 12. When was the GMT-Master introduced, and why?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion`.
- **Short answer:** The GMT-Master was introduced in 1955, designed for Pan Am pilots who needed to track two time zones simultaneously on long-haul transatlantic routes. The rotating 24-hour bezel plus a second time-zone hand allowed a quick read of home and destination time.
- **Evidence keys:** H10.

### 13. When was the Cosmograph Daytona introduced?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion`.
- **Short answer:** The Cosmograph Daytona was introduced in 1963, named for the Daytona International Speedway. It was built as a motorsport chronograph with a tachymeter bezel for calculating average speeds over a fixed distance. It sold slowly at first and only became a cultural phenomenon decades later.
- **Evidence keys:** H11.

### 14. What is the Explorer's connection to Mount Everest?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion` (frequently asked; historically contested).
- **Short answer:** Rolex equipped the 1953 British Mount Everest expedition, during which Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay reached the summit on 29 May 1953. The Explorer model was introduced the same year to commemorate the climb. Historians note that Norgay was definitely wearing a Rolex Oyster Perpetual on the climb; which watch Hillary personally wore at the summit is historically disputed (a Smith's watch is also commonly cited for Hillary).
- **Evidence keys:** H9.

### 15. When was the Sea-Dweller introduced, and what makes it different?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion`.
- **Short answer:** The Sea-Dweller was introduced in 1967. Its distinguishing feature is a helium escape valve (Rolex patent CH492246), which lets helium that has permeated the case during saturation diving vent safely during decompression, preventing the crystal from popping off on ascent. Initial rating was 2,000 ft (610 m), extended to 4,000 ft (1,220 m) in 1978.
- **Evidence keys:** H12.

### 16. What is the Deepsea Challenge?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion`.
- **Short answer:** The Deepsea Challenge (ref. 126067, released 2022) is a 50 mm titanium-cased Rolex rated to 11,000 m. It is the production evolution of an experimental model that accompanied James Cameron on his 2012 Challenger Deep dive. It is a specialist piece — most wrists will not wear a 50 mm, 23 mm-thick watch comfortably.
- **Evidence keys:** H13.

### 17. Who owns Rolex?
- **Origin:** `google-observed` — query "who owns Rolex" clusters tightly in search results (observed 2026-11-17 across https://www.luxuryofwatches.com/blog/who-owns-rolex/, https://tropicalwatch.com/p/journal/who-owns-rolex-and-why-it-s-unlike-any-other-watch-brand, https://watchmydiamonds.com/blog/post/who-really-owns-rolex/).
- **Short answer:** Rolex SA is 100% owned by the Hans Wilsdorf Foundation, a Swiss private foundation established by the founder in 1945 after his wife's death, with full company ownership transferred to it in 1960. Rolex SA itself is a for-profit manufacturer; its sole shareholder being a charitable foundation means the company is not stock-market traded and does not report profits publicly.
- **Evidence keys:** H14.

## Manufacture & technical questions

### 18. What is Oystersteel, and how is it different from ordinary stainless steel?
- **Origin:** `google-observed` — the phrasing is common to multiple articles including https://precisionwatches.com/journal/what-is-oystersteel/ and https://watchskins.sg/blogs/guides/what-is-oystersteel/ (observed 2026-11-17).
- **Short answer:** Oystersteel is Rolex's trade name (from 2018) for 904L stainless steel, used across all Rolex steel watches since Rolex completed the transition in 2003. Compared with the 316L steel used by much of the industry, 904L contains more chromium, nickel and molybdenum, which materially improves corrosion resistance — particularly to chloride environments like seawater and perspiration. It is harder to machine, which is why most of the industry sticks with 316L.
- **Evidence keys:** M1, M2, M3.

### 19. What does "Superlative Chronometer" mean on a Rolex dial?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion`.
- **Short answer:** Every modern Rolex is first certified as a chronometer by the Contrôle Officiel Suisse des Chronomètres (COSC), which guarantees -4/+6 seconds per day movement-only. Rolex then cases the movement and runs its own "Superlative Chronometer" test, which tightens the specification to an average -2/+2 seconds per day on the fully cased, fully wound watch. The five-year international warranty is tied to this certification.
- **Evidence keys:** M4.

### 20. What is the Parachrom hairspring?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion`.
- **Short answer:** Parachrom is Rolex's paramagnetic hairspring alloy, composed of niobium, zirconium and oxygen. It was introduced in 2000 and updated in 2005 to add the characteristic blue oxide finish. Compared with traditional ferromagnetic hairsprings, it is largely immune to magnetic-field disturbance, more stable across temperature swings, and reported by Rolex as up to ten times more shock-resistant.
- **Evidence keys:** M5.

### 21. What is the Chronergy escapement?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion`.
- **Short answer:** The Chronergy escapement is a Rolex-designed variant of the Swiss lever escapement, introduced in 2015 in the 3235/3255 calibre family and later in the 3285 (GMT-Master II). Rolex changes the geometry of the pallet and uses a ferromagnetically-neutral nickel-phosphorous alloy, resulting in approximately 15% higher energy efficiency, which contributes to the ~70-hour power reserve of modern calibres.
- **Evidence keys:** M7.

## Buying & ownership questions

### 22. How often should a Rolex be serviced?
- **Origin:** `google-observed` — phrasing matches https://www.bobswatches.com/rolex-blog/resources/how-often-should-you-service-your-rolex.html and https://watchrepairlasvegas.com/blog/rolex-service-interval/ (observed 2026-11-17).
- **Short answer:** Rolex's published guidance since 2016 is "approximately every ten years" for modern watches under normal use. That is an average. A daily-wear watch, especially a diver that sees salt water, benefits from earlier attention — many watchmakers suggest every 5-7 years. Vintage references from before 1990 typically want service every 3-5 years. Any sign of moisture under the crystal is a reason to stop wearing it and send it in immediately.
- **Evidence keys:** B1.

### 23. How much does a Rolex service cost in 2026?
- **Origin:** `google-observed` — the phrasing clusters around article titles "Rolex Service Cost 2026" (https://www.bobswatches.com/rolex-blog/editorial/how-much-to-service-a-rolex.html, https://www.luxuryofwatches.com/blog/rolex-service-cost) observed 2026-11-17.
- **Short answer:** At Rolex Service Centres in 2026, modern three-hand models (Datejust, Explorer, Air-King) run around $660 USD / £550 GBP; sports models (Submariner, GMT-Master II, Sea-Dweller) around $770 USD / £640 GBP; and the modern Daytona around $940 USD / £780 GBP. Classic five-digit and vintage four-digit references cost significantly more. Independent watchmakers may charge less but do not carry Rolex's own two-year post-service guarantee.
- **Evidence keys:** B2, B3.

### 24. Is a Rolex Submariner really safe to dive with?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion`.
- **Short answer:** A current Submariner is rated to 300 m (1,000 ft) under static lab testing, and Rolex tests each case to +10% of that. For recreational scuba diving, that rating has huge headroom. The honest caveats are (a) the lab rating is static; real-world wrist motion, pressure surges and gasket age all reduce the effective margin; (b) the watch needs to be pressure-tested at service intervals to keep the rating valid; and (c) hot showers and sauna heat stress gaskets more than diving does.
- **Evidence keys:** B4, B5.

### 25. What size Rolex fits my wrist?
- **Origin:** `google-observed` — query clusters around https://www.swisswatchexpo.com/thewatchclub/rolex-case-sizes/ and https://www.bobswatches.com/rolex-blog/editorial/36mm-vs-41mm-rolex.html (observed 2026-11-17).
- **Short answer:** Case diameter alone is a weak proxy — lug-to-lug (L2L) matters more. Rough sizing: wrists under 16.5 cm / 6.5 in sit best with 36 mm (≈ 44 mm L2L); 7-7.5 in / 17.8-19 cm wrists comfortably take either 36 mm or 40-41 mm; above 7.5 in / 19 cm, 40-41 mm tends to look balanced. Submariner Date is 41 mm / 47 mm L2L; Daytona is 40 mm / 48 mm L2L; GMT-Master II is 40 mm / 47 mm L2L. Try before you buy — proportions on paper can mislead.
- **Evidence keys:** B6.

### 26. How do I avoid a counterfeit when buying online?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion`.
- **Short answer:** The hard truth is that no online check gives certainty. Rolex does not operate a public serial-number lookup, and modern fakes routinely carry a copied serial from a real watch. The reliable test is physical movement inspection by a trained watchmaker, which no listing photo can replace. Practical risk-reducers: buy from a dealer who provides a written authenticity guarantee and a money-back return policy; favour Rolex Certified Pre-Owned (two-year manufacturer guarantee, authenticated at an authorised retailer); request high-resolution photos of the movement, caseback, rehaut engraving and lugs; and treat peer-to-peer platforms (eBay, Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace) as high-risk by default.
- **Evidence keys:** B7, MV8.

### 27. What is the Rolex Certified Pre-Owned programme?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion`.
- **Short answer:** Rolex's Certified Pre-Owned (CPO) programme launched in December 2022 — initially at Bucherer boutiques in six European countries, expanding to the US with Tourneau in May 2023. It authenticates second-hand Rolex watches that are at least three years old, backs them with a new two-year international manufacturer guarantee, and presents them with CPO-specific packaging and seal. The trade-off versus a non-CPO pre-owned purchase is that CPO prices are higher, but authentication risk is effectively eliminated.
- **Evidence keys:** MV8.

### 28. Did Rolex buy Bucherer?
- **Origin:** `google-observed` — the phrasing matches https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/business/rolex-breaks-taboo-with-takeover-of-bucherer/48833586 and https://rapaport.com/news/rolex-signs-deal-to-buy-watch-retailer-bucherer/ (observed 2026-11-17).
- **Short answer:** Yes. In August 2023, Rolex announced the acquisition of Bucherer, one of its largest retail partners, with terms undisclosed. Bucherer continues to operate under its own brand with its existing management, and Rolex stated that the rest of its authorised-retailer network is unaffected. The practical consequence is that Rolex now has direct retail distribution for the first time in its modern history.
- **Evidence keys:** MV9.

## Market-value questions

### 29. What is the Rolex waitlist like in 2026?
- **Origin:** `google-observed` — article-title cluster at https://www.luxurybazaar.com/grey-market/rolex-waitlist/, https://miojewelry.com/market-investment/rolex-waitlists-still-real-or-fading-myth/, and https://grailr.watch/blog/rolex-waiting-list-2026 (observed 2026-11-17).
- **Short answer:** Waitlists remain real in late 2026 but have compressed sharply from the 2021-2022 peak. Across most references the median authorised-dealer wait is reported at 1-3 months. The exceptions sit in the sports-steel tier: Submariner is reported at 1-3 years, and the Daytona remains the longest wait at 5-8 years. Waitlist data is self-reported by dealers and buyers, not audited, and varies by boutique and by purchase-history relationship with the dealer.
- **Evidence keys:** MV1.

### 30. Are Rolex prices going down?
- **Origin:** `google-observed` — the WatchCharts and Quill&Pad market-update pages consistently use this phrasing (observed 2026-11-17).
- **Short answer:** They moved both ways. After the 2022 peak, the WatchCharts Rolex index declined through 2023 and into early 2024 as grey-market supply caught up with demand and post-pandemic spending cooled. The market stabilised in 2024 and ticked up through 2025-2026. Rolex itself raised authorised-dealer retail prices by roughly 7% in January 2026, which compressed the gap between grey-market and retail on most references (Daytona excepted). Any statement about "Rolex prices" needs a date and a reference — the index behaves very differently from individual references, and dealer asking prices behave differently from completed sales.
- **Evidence keys:** MV1, MV2, MV3, MV4.

### 31. Is a Rolex a good investment?
- **Origin:** `google-observed` — the phrasing is explicitly the article-title cluster at https://www.suttonsandrobertsons.com/articles/is-a-rolex-a-good-investment, https://brite.co/blog/is-buying-a-rolex-a-good-investment/, https://www.luxmetrix.com/guides/rolex-investment and others (observed 2026-11-17).
- **Short answer:** Historically, certain Rolex references have appreciated over multi-year windows — the Submariner, GMT-Master II and Daytona are the most frequently cited. That history does not guarantee future performance. Compared with financial assets a Rolex has real costs that reduce net return: dealer spread on sale (often 10-20%), service every ~10 years, insurance, and the risk of a market correction like 2022-2024. The honest framing is that a well-chosen steel Rolex has historically held value better than most consumer goods, but it is a watch first and a store of value second — not an investment vehicle substitute.
- **Evidence keys:** MV1-MV7, MV10.

### 32. What are the hidden costs of owning a Rolex?
- **Origin:** `editorial-suggestion`.
- **Short answer:** Four items are routinely under-counted by first-time buyers: (1) Rolex Service Centre servicing at ~$660-$940 USD or ~£550-£780 GBP per cycle on modern references, roughly once per decade; (2) selling frictions — dealer spreads of 10-20% on resale, or auction-house fees around 15-25% buyer's premium; (3) scheduled valuation and insurance premiums, especially if value has moved since purchase; and (4) on vintage pieces, the risk that a routine Rolex Service Centre polish will reduce value — "no polish" is a request vintage owners learn to make.
- **Evidence keys:** B2, B3, MV7, MV10.
