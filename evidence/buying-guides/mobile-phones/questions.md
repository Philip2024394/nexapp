# Buyer Questions — Mobile Phones

**Compiled:** 2026-11-17
**Source discovery:** Google "People also ask", Reddit threads (r/phones, r/iPhone, r/Android, r/refurbishedphones), Which? and Consumer Reports reader-question sections, Tom's Guide and Apple Community thread titles.
**Tagging:** [Observed on Google] = seen as an actual search suggestion, "people also ask" entry or forum title. [Editorial] = added by the editor to fill a decision-framework gap, not directly observed.

Answers are 2–5 sentences, evergreen, neutral. Source IDs refer to `research-ledger.md`.

### 1. What should I check first on a used phone before I pay? [Observed on Google]
Before money changes hands, confirm three things in person: the IMEI shown by dialling *#06# matches the IMEI printed on the box or in Settings, the battery health reading from the phone's own settings menu, and that the device is not locked to the previous owner's account (no Activation Lock screen on iPhone, no Google account prompt on Android after a factory reset). If any of those three cannot be verified, walk away. (Sources: S3, S5, S17.)

### 2. What is a "clean" IMEI and why does it matter? [Observed on Google]
Every phone has a 15-digit IMEI defined by 3GPP standards that uniquely identifies the hardware on any mobile network. Carriers and the GSMA's Central Equipment Identity Register maintain a global list of IMEIs reported lost, stolen or with unpaid financing — a "clean" IMEI is one that does not appear on that list. A blocked IMEI can stop the phone from connecting to carriers in the country where it was reported, even after a factory reset. (Sources: S11, S12, S15.)

### 3. Is an IMEI check enough on its own? [Editorial]
No. BBC reporting has documented that IMEIs can be altered on some devices by sellers operating in the grey market, and a clean IMEI check reflects only what has been reported so far — a phone stolen hours ago may not yet appear. Treat the IMEI check as necessary but not sufficient: combine it with proof of purchase from the seller, in-person inspection and payment through a channel that lets you dispute. (Source: S17.)

### 4. What does "refurbished" actually mean? [Observed on Google]
"Refurbished" means a previously owned or returned device that a seller has inspected, tested, cleaned and (where needed) repaired before reselling, usually with a warranty. "Used" or "pre-owned" describes history only — no promise of testing or warranty. "Renewed" is a marketplace brand name (notably Amazon's) applied to devices that meet that marketplace's refurbishment standard. (Source: S1, S4.)

### 5. What do grades A, B, C mean on a refurbished phone? [Observed on Google]
Grades are a seller-defined cosmetic scale, not a legal standard, so always read the specific seller's grade definition. Broadly: Grade A is near-new in appearance, Grade B shows light marks visible on close inspection, Grade C shows visible wear but is fully functional. Which? has recommended Grade B as the usual balance between price and condition for most buyers. (Source: S1.)

### 6. How many years of software updates should I expect? [Observed on Google]
The EU's Ecodesign regulation for smartphones, in force from 20 June 2025, sets a floor of at least five years of operating-system and security updates after the last unit of a model is placed on the EU market. Google and Samsung currently publish seven-year commitments for their newer flagship ranges (Pixel 8 onward, Galaxy S24 onward), and Apple's practical pattern for iPhones has typically been six to eight years of combined major and security updates. Shorter-cycle Android brands often promise less — always check the specific model's published policy. (Sources: S7, S8, S10.)

### 7. Does buying a phone "unlocked" guarantee it will work on my carrier? [Observed on Google]
No. "Unlocked" means the software is not restricted to one carrier, but the radio hardware still has to support the frequency bands your carrier uses — particularly for 5G, where regional band support varies widely. A phone sold unlocked in another country may work for calls and basic LTE but fail to deliver 5G, VoLTE, Wi-Fi Calling or satellite services. Compare the phone's published band list against the carrier's deployed bands before buying. (Source: see external band references; Editorial synthesis.)

### 8. What does IP68 mean, and is my phone waterproof? [Observed on Google]
IP68 is a rating under the IEC 60529 standard: the first digit (6) means fully dust-tight, the second (8) means protection against continuous immersion beyond the conditions of IP67, under the manufacturer's declared depth and duration. It is a laboratory-tested resistance rating, not a waterproof guarantee, and it does not cover salt water, pressurised water (shower, hose), or water after the phone has aged, been dropped, or had the back opened for repair. Warranties generally exclude liquid damage even on IP68 devices. (Source: S13.)

### 9. How much storage do I really need? [Observed on Google]
Pick storage by workload, not by habit. If you stream most media and keep photos and video in a cloud service you already pay for, a 128 GB device is usually sufficient for two to three years. If you shoot 4K video, keep large offline music or map libraries, or play big games, plan for 256 GB or more. Remember the OS and pre-installed apps take roughly 15–20 GB before you store anything. (Source: S2.)

### 10. Do megapixels equal better photos? [Observed on Google]
No. Sensor size — the physical area of the image sensor — usually matters more than the megapixel count printed on the spec sheet, because a larger sensor gathers more light and produces cleaner images in dim conditions and wider dynamic range in bright ones. A well-designed 12-megapixel or 48-megapixel sensor can out-perform a 200-megapixel sensor in low light if the smaller-count sensor is larger overall. (Source: Editorial synthesis from independent testing coverage.)

### 11. What does "battery health" actually measure on a used phone? [Observed on Google]
It is an estimate of maximum charge capacity remaining compared to when the battery was new, expressed as a percentage. On iPhone it appears as "Maximum Capacity" in Settings › Battery › Battery Health & Charging; equivalent readouts exist in recent Pixel and Samsung One UI releases. Apple itself recommends battery service around 80% maximum capacity, which is a useful threshold to apply across brands even if the exact number is manufacturer-specific. (Sources: S19, S20, S21.)

### 12. What battery health figure is "good enough" on a used phone? [Observed on Google]
As a rule of thumb, 90% or higher is strong, 85–89% is acceptable for a phone you plan to keep 12–18 months, and below 80% you should either negotiate for a replacement battery or budget to replace the battery yourself. A swollen or bulging back cover is a hard "no" regardless of the figure — that is a safety issue, not a condition one. (Sources: S19, S24.)

### 13. Does Apple's warranty transfer when I buy a used iPhone? [Observed on Google]
Yes, for the remaining portion of the one-year Apple Limited Warranty, which runs from the original retail sale date (not the date you bought it second-hand) and attaches to the serial number. AppleCare+ is a different product — on legacy single-payment plans it typically transfers with the device, while monthly AppleCare+ stays with the original account and does not move to a new owner. Check coverage using Apple's online coverage checker before you pay. (Source: S5 and Apple Support linked threads.)

### 14. What is Activation Lock and how do I know it is off? [Observed on Google]
Activation Lock ties an iPhone or iPad to the previous owner's Apple Account via Find My. If it is still on, the next person who tries to set the device up sees a "locked to owner" screen and cannot proceed. The only reliable way to confirm it is off is to have the seller fully erase the phone in front of you and watch it complete setup to the home screen without asking for anyone else's Apple Account. (Source: S5.)

### 15. How do I check an Android phone is not locked to someone else's Google account? [Editorial]
On Android this protection is called Factory Reset Protection. After a factory reset, the device will ask for the Google account that was last signed in — if the seller cannot pass that screen, do not buy. The same principle as Activation Lock: watch a complete factory reset through to a fresh home screen before paying. (Editorial, drawing on manufacturer policy.)

### 16. Should I buy a phone from another country to save money? [Observed on Google]
Sometimes yes for simple use cases, often no for modern 5G use. Beyond carrier band mismatch, imported phones can have region-locked features, missing local warranty cover, different charger plug standards and — in some markets — regulatory-mandated audio or camera-shutter behaviour that is not switchable. Confirm that your destination carrier's priority bands and services (VoLTE, Wi-Fi Calling, eSIM, satellite) are supported on the exact imported model before buying. (Source: Editorial synthesis.)

### 17. eSIM or physical SIM — which should I pick? [Observed on Google]
For a phone you keep in one country with one carrier, both work fine; the practical differences are swapping and travel. eSIM makes changing carriers or adding travel data lines faster, and it removes the SIM-tray attack path used in some SIM-swap fraud. Physical SIM is still easier if you hop between devices often. Note that a factory reset removes eSIM profiles, while a physical SIM retains its identity in the card itself. (Source: S22.)

### 18. What is "repairability" and why should I care before buying? [Observed on Google]
Repairability measures how easily common parts — chiefly the battery and screen — can be replaced without destroying the device. iFixit's public scoring, and independent academic review of that scoring, both identify battery access as the single biggest driver: phones with batteries held by screws or pull-tabs score higher than phones with batteries glued in place. A more repairable phone tends to be cheaper to keep alive past year three. (Sources: S9, S23.)

### 19. How long is a phone "supposed to" last? [Observed on Google]
Physically, five to seven years is realistic for a well-cared-for device whose battery is replaced once. The limiting factor is usually software: once the manufacturer stops shipping security updates, the phone is still functional but no longer safe for banking, email and account recovery. Match your intended keep-length to the model's published security-update end date — not to its launch date. (Sources: S7, S8, S10, S16.)

### 20. Can a refurbished phone be as good as new? [Observed on Google]
For the typical buyer, yes — provided it comes from a seller who publishes a grade definition, offers a warranty of at least 12 months, and discloses whether the battery has been replaced. Consumer Reports' review of the category concludes that refurbished devices from reputable channels generally match new devices for everyday reliability, while costing less. The gap is usually cosmetic, not functional. (Source: S4.)

### 21. What accessories should come with a used phone? [Editorial]
At minimum: a working charging cable and, if the device originally shipped with one, a compatible adapter. Inspect the cable for fraying and the connector for discolouration or bent pins. A missing original box is normal on a used device and is not itself a red flag; a missing or obviously third-party charger on a phone that originally included a specific one is worth asking about. (Editorial.)

### 22. How do I test a used phone's screen in five minutes? [Observed on Google]
Open a plain white image and inspect for dead pixels, yellow patches, or shadowy marks left by burn-in (common on older OLED screens). Open a plain black image and look for backlight bleed on LCD panels or stuck pixels on OLED. Run a finger slowly across the entire surface in a grid pattern on a drawing or notes app to confirm touch response at every point, including the corners. (Source: S14 principle; S24.)

### 23. Are there any phones I should avoid buying used? [Editorial]
Avoid any device whose manufacturer security-update window has already closed — the phone is cheap for a reason. Avoid handsets with known structural failures (search for the exact model plus "common faults" on a mainstream tech site), devices sold with the back cover already replaced by a non-OEM part (parts-pairing warnings may appear in Settings on recent iPhones), and any phone a seller refuses to let you power on and inspect before buying. (Source: S6.)

### 24. Does a "parts not genuine" warning matter? [Editorial]
On recent iPhones, Apple's parts-pairing system shows a notice in Settings when a replaced component (screen, battery, camera) is not recognised as a genuine part installed through an approved process. The phone still works, but some features (True Tone, battery health reporting, Face ID, camera modes) may be partially disabled. Treat the presence of this notice as a negotiation point — it indicates past repair history that the seller should disclose. (Source: S6.)

### 25. What is the difference between water-resistant and water-damaged? [Editorial]
Water resistance (IP67 / IP68) is a sealed-condition rating granted to a new device in a controlled test. Water damage happens after real-world exposure — swim, shower, splash — once gaskets have aged, the device has been opened for repair, or a drop has compromised the seal. A used phone with an IP68 rating on paper has unknown real-world sealing integrity, so do not assume the rating still applies. (Source: S13.)

### 26. Is a bigger screen always better? [Observed on Google]
No — it is a usability trade-off, not a quality trade-off. Larger screens fit more content and are better for video and reading; smaller screens fit better in one hand and in pockets, weigh less, and usually have better battery life per hour of use at the same brightness. Pick for your actual daily grip and bag, not for spec-sheet appeal. (Source: S2.)

### 27. How do I know if a phone's 5G is the "real" 5G my carrier offers? [Editorial]
Carriers deploy 5G on different frequency bands — sub-6 GHz bands (most common globally) and mmWave bands (mostly US urban). Cross-check the phone's declared 5G band list against the bands your carrier lists on its coverage page. If the phone lacks the bands your carrier prioritises in your area, you will connect but at LTE-like speeds. (Editorial.)

### 28. Should I care about the processor name? [Observed on Google]
Less than marketing implies. For everyday messaging, browsing, video and social-media use, a mid-tier processor from the last three years handles the load comfortably. Chip generation matters most for sustained gaming, 4K video editing and on-device AI features. Battery efficiency usually tracks with newer silicon, so a newer mid-tier chip often beats an older flagship for daily life. (Source: Editorial.)

### 29. What should a return policy on a used phone look like? [Editorial]
At minimum, a written return window of 14 days from receipt, with a defined process for defects discovered after setup, and clarity on who pays return postage. Platform-level buyer protection (card chargeback, escrow, marketplace guarantee) is more valuable than a seller's verbal promise. For in-person cash sales, there is no practical return — inspect before paying. (Source: S3.)

### 30. Is an older flagship better than a new mid-range? [Observed on Google]
Often yes on camera, display and build; often no on software-support remaining life, battery health and 5G band support. Compare what matters to you: if you want the best photo quality for the money and plan to keep the device 12–18 months, a one- or two-generation-old flagship is usually a strong buy. If you want the longest safe life, a new mid-range with a published long-support window may serve you longer. (Sources: S2, S7, S8.)

### 31. What questions should I ask the seller before buying? [Editorial]
Six questions: what is the IMEI (verified in person), has the battery ever been replaced, has the screen ever been replaced, is the device fully paid off and unlocked, is it still within the original manufacturer warranty, and is Find My / Factory Reset Protection turned off. A seller who answers all six confidently is a safer counterparty than one who hedges. (Sources: S3, S5.)

### 32. What are the red flags in a used-phone listing? [Observed on Google]
Photos that do not include a shot of the phone powered on, prices well below the current market for the model and condition, sellers who insist on payment methods with no buyer protection, listings that lack an IMEI or refuse to share one on request, and any claim of "iCloud locked but easily removable" or similar. Walking away costs nothing. (Source: S17.)
