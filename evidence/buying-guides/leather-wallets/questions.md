# Buyer questions — Leather wallets

Compiled: 2026-11-17
Tag key: [Observed] = seen in r/leathercraft, r/EDC, or Google "People also ask" for leather-wallet queries; [Editorial] = added for completeness. Answers are 2–5 sentences. Sources cross-referenced to `research-ledger.md`.

---

**Q1. What does "full-grain leather" actually mean? [Observed — r/leathercraft wiki, S21]**
Full-grain is the outermost layer of the hide with the natural grain surface intact — no sanding or buffing to remove scars or pores (S3, S8). In wallets it is the most durable option because the tightly packed fibre structure sits right at the surface where your fingers and pockets wear the material. Over time it develops patina rather than flaking.

**Q2. Is "genuine leather" good or bad? [Observed — People also ask]**
"Genuine leather" is a labelling term, not a quality grade. Under US FTC guides and ISO vocabulary it only confirms the product is real leather; it is often applied to lower-quality splits or heavily corrected hides (S3, S5). For a wallet, treat "genuine leather" as the floor, not a selling point.

**Q3. What is bonded leather and why do people warn against it? [Observed — r/EDC, S22]**
Bonded leather is shredded leather fibres mixed with a polyurethane or latex binder, then rolled into sheets and embossed with a grain pattern (S3, S5). It can look like leather for a few months but typically delaminates and flakes along fold lines. US FTC guides require it to be labelled with the percentage of leather content (S5).

**Q4. Vegetable-tanned vs chrome-tanned — which is better for a wallet? [Observed — r/leathercraft]**
Neither is universally "better." Vegetable-tanned leather develops dramatic patina, feels firmer, and tools well, which suits cardholders and minimalist bifolds that are meant to age (S9, S25). Chrome-tanned is softer, more water-tolerant and holds dyed colour more stably, which suits zip-arounds and long wallets that flex and travel.

**Q5. Does vegetable-tanned leather really develop patina? [Observed — r/leathercraft]**
Yes. The plant tannins oxidise on exposure to UV, skin oils and air, darkening and warming the surface over months to years (S9, S25). The effect is strongest on undyed or lightly dyed veg-tan and minimal on heavily finished or pigmented leathers.

**Q6. What is top-grain leather? [Editorial]**
Top-grain is the upper layer of the hide where the very top surface has been sanded or buffed to remove imperfections, often then embossed with a uniform grain (S3, S8). It is more uniform than full-grain but slightly less durable because the tightest fibre layer has been abraded away.

**Q7. What is a "split"? [Editorial]**
When a hide is horizontally split, the lower layer (the "split") lacks the grain surface. It is often finished with polymer coatings to imitate top-grain, and under ISO vocabulary this must be disclosed (S3, S5). Splits are acceptable for linings but a weak choice for the outer panels of a wallet.

**Q8. Is suede the same as nubuck? [Observed — People also ask]**
No. Nubuck is top-grain leather with the grain side lightly buffed to create a velvety nap, while suede is a split with the flesh side raised (S3, S8). Nubuck is stronger but both are vulnerable to staining and darkening from pocket oils — a practical issue for wallets.

**Q9. What is corrected-grain leather? [Editorial]**
Corrected-grain has the natural grain sanded off and replaced with an embossed pattern and a pigment layer (S8, S10). It gives a uniform look but the surface coating can crack along fold creases in a bifold, so it is more forgiving on flat cardholders than on wallets with heavy folding.

**Q10. What is patent leather and does it suit a wallet? [Editorial]**
Patent leather has a high-gloss plastic or lacquer coating on the surface (S3, S8). The coating resists water but is prone to scuff and crease marks, and once cracked it cannot be refinished at home — a poor match for a wallet that lives in a pocket.

**Q11. Is vegan/PU leather durable enough for a wallet? [Observed — r/EDC]**
Modern polyurethane (PU) wallets can be perfectly functional for a few years, but PU surface layers hydrolyse over time and typically crack or peel at fold points earlier than quality leather (S23, S4). Newer plant-based materials (cactus, mushroom, apple) still use polymer binders, so independent longevity data is limited.

**Q12. What is saddle stitching and why do craft wallets advertise it? [Observed — r/leathercraft]**
Saddle stitch uses two needles passing through each hole from opposite sides, so each stitch is an independent knot (S19). If one thread breaks, the stitch next to it does not unravel — unlike a machine lock-stitch, which can zip open once a single loop fails. It is slower and more expensive, which is why hand-saddle-stitched wallets cost more.

**Q13. What does SPI (stitches per inch) tell me? [Observed — Carryology, S18]**
SPI measures stitch density. On a wallet, 7–10 SPI is typical for veg-tan hand stitching, 8–12 SPI for machine stitching on thinner leathers (S18, S19). Very low SPI on thin leather looks sparse and lets the thread chafe; very high SPI on thick leather perforates the panel like a tear-strip.

**Q14. How can I tell if the edges are burnished or painted? [Editorial]**
A burnished edge is the leather itself, compressed and polished until it is smooth and slightly glossy — you can see the fibre layers (S20). A painted edge is a layer of acrylic or polyurethane finish laid on top; it looks uniform in colour and can chip. Painted edges are faster to produce and common on chrome-tanned wallets; burnished edges are typical of veg-tan.

**Q15. What is a "turned edge" vs a "raw edge"? [Editorial]**
On a turned edge the leather is skived thin at the perimeter, folded back onto itself, and stitched — hiding the raw edge inside (common on dress bifolds). On a raw edge the leather is cut, sealed and left exposed (common on craft bifolds and cardholders). Turned edges look refined but hide stitch stress from inspection; raw edges are easier to judge by eye.

**Q16. Why do card slots fail first? [Observed — r/EDC]**
Card slots take the most friction from repeated card insertion, and the top edge of a slot is a stress riser for the stitching next to it. Skived (thinned) and folded card-slot leather reduces bulk but can tear if the skive is too thin (S8, S19). Look for a short run of backstitching at each end of the slot.

**Q17. Does RFID shielding actually protect my cards? [Observed — People also ask]**
A metal or metallised-fabric layer in the wallet does attenuate 13.56 MHz signals used by contactless EMV cards (S14). However, modern contactless payment cards generate a one-time cryptogram per transaction, so a passive skim cannot be replayed to make a fraudulent purchase (S15). The practical fraud rate from contactless skimming is very low compared to magstripe cloning (S16, S17).

**Q18. So is RFID shielding pointless? [Editorial]**
No, but its real value is narrower than marketing suggests. Shielding is most useful for (a) access-control cards and transit cards that may use weaker authentication, and (b) preventing accidental double-reads at payment terminals (S14, S17). For payment cards, it is a belt-and-braces measure rather than a necessary one.

**Q19. What about relay attacks? [Editorial]**
Relay attacks bridge a card and a terminal across a longer distance using radio or network equipment (S17). They are a real research topic but require specialised hardware and proximity to the victim's card and a cooperating terminal. A shielded wallet does defeat these by blocking the radio link at the card end.

**Q20. How do I break in a new leather wallet? [Observed — r/leathercraft]**
Use it. Natural flexing from carrying it and loading/unloading cards softens the fibres at the fold within a few weeks for chrome-tanned leather and a few months for firm veg-tan. Avoid soaking, heating or "oiling heavily" at purchase — too much conditioner at the start plasticises the fibres and causes sagging.

**Q21. How often should I condition leather? [Observed — r/leathercraft]**
For most wallets, a light application of a neutral leather conditioner one to three times per year is enough (S9, S10). Chrome-tanned leathers need less than veg-tan. Over-conditioning darkens the surface permanently, softens the structure, and can bleed through onto card faces.

**Q22. My wallet got wet — what do I do? [Observed — r/EDC]**
Blot (don't rub) with a cloth, open the wallet, remove cards, and let it air-dry away from direct heat (S9, S10). Heat sources cause the collagen fibres to shrink and the leather to crack. Once fully dry, apply a light conditioner to restore flexibility.

**Q23. How do I clean a leather wallet? [Editorial]**
For general dirt, a barely damp microfibre cloth and a drop of pH-neutral soap is enough. Avoid baby wipes, alcohol, and saddle soap on finished leathers — all can strip dye or finish. For suede and nubuck use a dedicated nap brush; water and oils will stain them visibly.

**Q24. How do I spot a near end-of-life wallet? [Editorial]**
Watch for: cracked or flaking surface at the fold crease (especially on corrected-grain or PU), stitches that have broken on more than one card slot, edge paint chipping away revealing untreated leather, and sagging card slots that no longer grip. Replacing stitching can extend life; delaminating surface layers usually cannot be fixed.

**Q25. Is thicker leather always better? [Observed — r/leathercraft]**
No. Wallet leather is usually 1.0–1.6 mm (roughly 2.5–4 oz in craft measurement) (S8). Thicker leather is more durable but adds bulk and resists folding — a 3 mm bifold will feel like a brick and strain its own stitching at the spine. Thinner leather is fine if the fibre is dense and the construction is sound.

**Q26. Bifold vs cardholder vs long wallet vs zip-around — how do I choose? [Observed — r/EDC]**
Cardholders prioritise slimness and front-pocket carry (typically 4–8 cards, no cash fold). Bifolds balance capacity and size. Long wallets lay cash flat and suit larger bags or jacket pockets. Zip-arounds secure contents (useful for travel) but add bulk and introduce a zip as a wear point.

**Q27. Why does my veg-tan cardholder get darker on one side? [Observed — r/leathercraft]**
Uneven patina is normal — the side that faces outward, sees sunlight, or contacts skin darkens faster (S9, S25). To even it out, rotate how you carry it for a few weeks or leave it face-up in indirect daylight. Attempting to "fix" the colour with dye usually makes it worse.

**Q28. Should I buy an RFID-blocking cardholder? [Observed — People also ask]**
Only if (a) you also carry access, transit or ID cards that use RFID with weaker authentication, or (b) you want the extra peace of mind for very little downside. For EMV payment cards alone, the cryptographic protections in the card itself already mitigate most contactless fraud (S15, S17).

**Q29. Can I tell real leather from fake at a glance? [Observed — People also ask]**
Not reliably with embossed splits and modern PU. Look at the cut edges (real leather shows fibrous layers; PU looks like plastic foam), smell (real leather has an organic smell; PU smells faintly chemical), and check the label — US and UK regulations require accurate material disclosure (S3, S4, S5, S6). Microscopy is the definitive test (S4) but obviously not pocket-friendly.

**Q30. How long should a good leather wallet last? [Observed — r/EDC]**
With average daily use, a full-grain wallet with solid stitching commonly lasts 5–15 years before needing repair or replacement; cardholders often outlast bifolds because they flex less. PU-based wallets commonly show cracking at folds within 1–3 years (S23). These are rough ranges — pocket environment, climate and card load all matter.
